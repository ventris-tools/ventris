Help

Ventris shows the signals and frequency bands of an EEG recording, right in your browser.

Your recordings stay with you

Everything runs in your browser, so your recordings never leave your computer. Nothing is uploaded.

Opening a public recording by link is the one time Ventris uses the network. It downloads that file and nothing else. Amazon and GitHub see your IP address and which file you asked for, as they would if you downloaded it yourself.

Open a recording

Select Open a recording and choose an EDF, EDF+ or CSV file, or drop a file anywhere on the page.

The whole file is read into your browser's memory, so a very large recording may not open.

Open a public recording by link

Ventris can open a recording from a NEMAR dataset straight from GitHub. Find the file in a repository of the nemarDatasets organization, copy its address, and paste it under Open a recording. The address looks like this:

https://github.com/nemarDatasets/on003194/blob/main/sub-NEPO01/ses-Nepopre/eeg/sub-NEPO01_ses-Nepopre_task-taskprenepo_eeg.edf

A link to the same file in OpenNeuro's public bucket, starting https://s3.amazonaws.com/openneuro.org/, works too.

GitHub keeps only a small pointer for each recording, so Ventris downloads the file from OpenNeuro's copy and checks it against the size and the MD5 or SHA hash in the pointer. What you see is the version the link names.

Once it opens, the address bar links to the recording, so you can share the view or come back to it.

Datasets starting onNEMAR mirrors these from OpenNeuro, and they open.
Datasets starting nmNEMAR's own datasets don't open yet. Their files are public, but their storage doesn't let a web page download them.
File typesEDF only. Link to one recording, a file ending in .edf, not to a dataset or a folder.
SizeUp to 1 GB. Download a larger recording and open it as a file.

CSV files

A CSV file needs a header row that names its columns, then one row per sample. Lab Streaming Layer recordings are often saved this way, and so is MNE-Python's to_data_frame(). Files split by tabs or semicolons work too.

TimeA column named time or timestamp, in seconds, or in milliseconds when the name ends in ms. A file without one can't be opened.
ChannelsEvery other column of numbers. A unit at the end of a name, as in Fz [uV], becomes the channel's unit. Without one, values show as they are in the file.
EventsA column that is empty in most rows, such as a marker stream. Each value in it becomes an annotation.
Left outRow numbers, and columns of words such as formatted dates.

The sample rate comes from the usual step between times. A rate within 0.5% of one that devices use, such as 250.25 Hz for 250 Hz, counts as that rate. If the times jump anywhere, a note says the recording has gaps.

A CSV says nothing about filters or the participant, so those show as Unknown. Info gives the first timestamp, which lines up files from one session.

The demo

Ventris opens on a demo. It's generated data for trying out Ventris, not a real recording.

A blink shows in Fp1 and Fp2, and alpha builds at the back of the head when the eyes close at 0:20.

Read the traces

Each row is one channel. The ± figure under a channel's name is how far that row reaches either side of its middle.

Each row has its own ± range, so compare shapes rather than heights.

Point at a trace to read its value at that moment. With a display filter on, that's the filtered value.

Move through a recording

WindowSets how much of the recording is on screen.
Trace sizeMakes every trace smaller or larger. The ± figures change to match.
SliderMoves through the recording.
SwipeA sideways swipe on the traces moves through time. So does holding Shift while you scroll.

Zoom in

Drag a box across the traces with the mouse to zoom to that stretch of time and those channels. The channels you keep share the height of the view, so their traces grow.

A flat drag zooms time alone, and a tall, narrow drag zooms channels alone. Drag down the channel names to pick channels.

While you're zoomed, the slider, swipes and keys move through time as usual, and the Window list says Zoomed.

Double-click the traces, press Escape or select Reset zoom to see the whole window and every channel again. If you haven't moved, you're back where you started.

Display filter

The Display filter lists filter the traces as they're drawn. The file never changes, and the line under the traces says which filters are on.

High-passTakes out offsets and slow drift below the frequency you choose.
Low-passTakes out fast activity above the frequency you choose, such as muscle. EMG channels keep theirs, since that's what they measure.
NotchTakes out mains hum at 50 or 60 Hz, though not its harmonics.
MontageRe-references the EEG channels. Average takes the mean of them all away from each. Laplacian takes the mean of each electrode's nearest neighbours away from it, which sharpens activity under the electrode, such as the mu rhythm over C3 and C4.
Show rawDraws the samples as recorded, to compare. Select it again, or press R, to go back to the filtered traces.

Each filter runs forwards and then backwards, so nothing shifts in time and peaks stay under their annotations. Running twice halves the signal at the cutoff frequency, as MNE-Python's IIR filters do. The high-pass and low-pass are second-order Butterworth filters, and the notch is the one SciPy's iirnotch makes, with a Q of 30.

Channels from sensors, such as triggers, breathing belts and oximeters, stay as recorded, and the line under the traces says how many. A filter too close to half a channel's sample rate is left out for that channel.

A montage runs after the filters, on EEG channels sampled at the same rate. It leaves out bipolar pairs such as Fp1-F7, ear and mastoid references, flat channels and every other kind of channel. A Laplacian finds neighbours from 10-10 electrode names, so it leaves out any channel whose name it doesn't know, and any with fewer than two neighbours. Each row a montage changes says so after its name, as in C3−avg or C3−lap, and the line under the traces counts the EEG channels it left alone.

The traces, their ± figures, the value you point at and the frequency bands follow the filter. The time domain figures and Info describe the file as recorded.

Filters stay on when you open another recording. Undo a change of filter with Ctrl Z, or Cmd Z on a Mac, and redo it by adding Shift.

Gaps in a recording are filtered as if they weren't there, so a filter can ring for a few seconds after one. A recording with more than 150 million samples to filter stays raw.

Arrow keys on the sliderMove a tenth of a window.
EscapeUndo a zoom.
RSwitch between the filtered and the raw traces.
Ctrl ZUndo a change of filter, from anywhere on the page. Add Shift to redo. On a Mac, use Cmd.

Frequency bands

Each bar is one band's share of the 1 to 30 Hz power in the window on screen, from Welch spectra averaged across the EEG channels. The bars update as you move.

The line under the bars gives that range and how many channels were averaged. Point at a row for a more exact figure.

Only EEG channels sampled at 64 Hz or faster are measured. If bands can't be measured, the line says why.

With a display filter on, the bands measure the filtered traces, and the line says so.

Annotations

Annotations in an EDF+ file, and events in a CSV file, appear on the time axis. Select one in the list to jump to it.

The four commonest kinds of annotation each get a colour, on the traces and in the list. The colours stay apart for colour-blind readers too. Any further kinds are grey, and every annotation keeps its name.

Event-related power

This section shows how the power in a band falls or rises around one kind of annotation, averaged over its events. A fall is an event-related desynchronization (ERD), and a rise an event-related synchronization (ERS). The mu and beta rhythms over the motor cortex fall when a hand moves, or is imagined moving, most of all on the other side of the head: at C4 for the left hand and C3 for the right. A difference too small to see in single trials can show in the average.

Open the section, then choose:

AverageThe kinds of annotation to average, each drawn as a curve of its own. Kinds you choose stay chosen for the next recording that has them. Otherwise every kind with a colour starts chosen.
Band8 to 30 Hz, which takes in both rhythms, or mu, 8 to 13 Hz, or beta, 13 to 30 Hz.
BaselineWhat 0 dB stands for: the power in the second before the events, or the power during another kind of annotation, such as rest. A rest annotation counts from its onset for its duration, or for 4.5 s if it has none.
ChannelsThe channels to chart, such as C3 C4, by label or by the electrode a label names, so C3 finds C3.. and EEG C3-A2. Separate names with commas if they have spaces in them. Left empty, the chart follows the row the cursor rests on.

Each event's epoch runs from 1 s before its onset to 4.5 s after. The signal is band-passed, squared, smoothed over half a second and averaged across the events, then given in decibels against the baseline, where −3 dB is half the power. An event too near either end of the recording for a whole epoch is left out. The band-pass is a fourth-order Butterworth high-pass and low-pass, run forwards and backwards like the display filter, over each epoch and a second of the recording on either side.

Each curve has the colour of its annotations, and dashes of its own. The key under each chart names the kinds, counts the events averaged, and gives each kind's mean from 0.5 to 3.5 s after the onset, which leaves out the response to the onset itself. The charts share a scale, so their heights compare.

It measures the traces as drawn, with the display filter and montage, or as recorded while Show raw is on, and the line under the charts says which. A Laplacian montage brings out the mu rhythm under C3 and C4. A band reaching 30 Hz needs a sample rate above 66.7 Hz.

Ventris opens one file at a time, so it averages only that file's events. A motor imagery run of PhysioNet's EEG Motor Movement/Imagery dataset holds 7 or 8 events of each kind, so expect a noisy average, where a few dB either way can be chance. Studies average many more, over several runs.

Time domain

These figures cover the whole recording as recorded, pooled across the EEG channels. The line under them says how many, and says "as recorded" while a display filter is on. EEG channels in a different unit from the first one are left out.

Overall SDThe standard deviation of all those samples together.
Lowest SD, Highest SDThe channels with the smallest and largest standard deviation, leaving out flat ones.
Flat channelsHow many channels hold one value throughout.

Info

These details come from the file's header. For an EDF file they follow MNE-Python's EDF reader, so they usually match mne.io.read_raw_edf.

EOG, EMG and ECG channels are read from their labels and stay out of the EEG measures.

Light and dark

Ventris follows your system's theme until you pick one with the switch next to Help. Your choice stays in this browser.

Limits

  • Ventris is a research tool, not a medical device.
  • BDF files aren't supported yet.
  • It doesn't export signals.
