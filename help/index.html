Help

Ventris shows the signals and frequency bands of an EEG recording, right in your browser.

Your recordings stay with you

Everything runs in your browser, so your recordings never leave your computer. Nothing is uploaded.

Opening a public recording by link is the one time Ventris uses the network. It downloads that file and nothing else. Amazon and GitHub see your IP address and which file you asked for, as they would if you downloaded it yourself.

Open a recording

Select Open a recording and choose an EDF, EDF+ or CSV file, or drop a file anywhere on the page.

The whole file is read into your browser's memory, so a very large recording may not open.

Open a public recording by link

Ventris can open a recording from a NEMAR dataset straight from GitHub. Find the file in a repository of the nemarDatasets organization, copy its address, and paste it under Open a recording. The address looks like this:

https://github.com/nemarDatasets/on003194/blob/main/sub-NEPO01/ses-Nepopre/eeg/sub-NEPO01_ses-Nepopre_task-taskprenepo_eeg.edf

A link to the same file in OpenNeuro's public bucket, starting https://s3.amazonaws.com/openneuro.org/, works too.

GitHub keeps only a small pointer for each recording, so Ventris downloads the file from OpenNeuro's copy and checks it against the size and the MD5 or SHA hash in the pointer. What you see is the version the link names.

Once it opens, the address bar links to the recording, so you can share the view or come back to it.

Datasets starting onNEMAR mirrors these from OpenNeuro, and they open.
Datasets starting nmNEMAR's own datasets don't open yet. Their files are public, but their storage doesn't let a web page download them.
File typesEDF only. Link to one recording, a file ending in .edf, not to a dataset or a folder.
SizeUp to 1 GB. Download a larger recording and open it as a file.

CSV files

A CSV file needs a header row that names its columns, then one row per sample. Lab Streaming Layer recordings are often saved this way, and so is MNE-Python's to_data_frame(). Files split by tabs or semicolons work too.

TimeA column named time or timestamp, in seconds, or in milliseconds when the name ends in ms. A file without one can't be opened.
ChannelsEvery other column of numbers. A unit at the end of a name, as in Fz [uV], becomes the channel's unit. Without one, values show as they are in the file.
EventsA column that is empty in most rows, such as a marker stream. Each value in it becomes an annotation.
Left outRow numbers, and columns of words such as formatted dates.

The sample rate comes from the usual step between times. A rate within 0.5% of one that devices use, such as 250.25 Hz for 250 Hz, counts as that rate. If the times jump anywhere, a note says the recording has gaps.

A CSV says nothing about filters or the participant, so those show as Unknown. Info gives the first timestamp, which lines up files from one session.

The demo

Ventris opens on a demo. It's generated data for trying out Ventris, not a real recording.

A blink shows in Fp1 and Fp2, and alpha builds at the back of the head when the eyes close at 0:20.

Read the traces

Each row is one channel. The ± figure under a channel's name is how far that row reaches either side of its middle.

Each row has its own ± range, so compare shapes rather than heights.

Point at a trace to read the recorded value at that moment.

Move through a recording

WindowSets how much of the recording is on screen.
Trace sizeMakes every trace smaller or larger. The ± figures change to match.
SliderMoves through the recording.
SwipeA sideways swipe on the traces moves through time. So does holding Shift while you scroll.

Zoom in

Drag a box across the traces with the mouse to zoom to that stretch of time and those channels. The channels you keep share the height of the view, so their traces grow.

A flat drag zooms time alone, and a tall, narrow drag zooms channels alone. Drag down the channel names to pick channels.

While you're zoomed, the slider, swipes and keys move through time as usual, and the Window list says Zoomed.

Double-click the traces, press Escape or select Reset zoom to see the whole window and every channel again. If you haven't moved, you're back where you started.

Keyboard

Select the traces or the slider first.

Page Up Page DownMove one window later or earlier.
Home EndGo to the start or the end.
Arrow keys on the tracesMove the cursor to read values: left and right through time, up and down between channels.
Arrow keys on the sliderMove a tenth of a window.
EscapeUndo a zoom.

Frequency bands

Each bar is one band's share of the 1 to 30 Hz power in the window on screen, from Welch spectra averaged across the EEG channels. The bars update as you move.

The line under the bars gives that range and how many channels were averaged. Point at a row for a more exact figure.

Only EEG channels sampled at 64 Hz or faster are measured. If bands can't be measured, the line says why.

Annotations

Annotations in an EDF+ file, and events in a CSV file, appear on the time axis. Select one in the list to jump to it.

The four commonest kinds of annotation each get a colour, on the traces and in the list. The colours stay apart for colour-blind readers too. Any further kinds are grey, and every annotation keeps its name.

Time domain

These figures cover the whole recording, pooled across the EEG channels. The line under them says how many. EEG channels in a different unit from the first one are left out.

Overall SDThe standard deviation of all those samples together.
Lowest SD, Highest SDThe channels with the smallest and largest standard deviation, leaving out flat ones.
Flat channelsHow many channels hold one value throughout.

Info

These details come from the file's header. For an EDF file they follow MNE-Python's EDF reader, so they usually match mne.io.read_raw_edf.

EOG, EMG and ECG channels are read from their labels and stay out of the EEG measures.

Light and dark

Ventris follows your system's theme until you pick one with the switch next to Help. Your choice stays in this browser.

Limits

  • Ventris is a research tool, not a medical device.
  • BDF files aren't supported yet.
  • It doesn't filter, re-reference or export signals.
